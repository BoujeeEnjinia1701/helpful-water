# Helpful Water source and use-environment classes

Each solution is designed for named source classes only; no solution is suitable for every water source. The classes and their implications come from the Rev A component specification (2026-10-14).

Table 1. Source classes and solution suitability (S suitable for design, C conditional on source-specific screening, X excluded).

| Class | Description | Hazards to screen | A straw | B bottle | C gravity | D add-on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SC1 Treated municipal tap | Chlorinated network water, often distrusted or unpalatable | Taste and odour; lead and PFAS are not removed | S (taste) | S (taste) | S | Not needed |
| SC2 Clear stream or lake | Low turbidity surface water | Microbes, seasonal turbidity, algae and cyanotoxins, upstream sewage | S | S | S | Use where viral risk is known |
| SC3 Moderately turbid river | 10 to 100 NTU | Fast fouling; viruses | C (pre-settle) | C (pre-settle) | S (settling stage) | C (clarify first) |
| SC4 Private or shallow well | Groundwater of unknown quality | E. coli, nitrate, arsenic, fluoride, metals | C after certified analysis | C | C | C |
| SC5 Stored household water | Water contaminated in handling and storage | Recontamination of containers | S | S | S | S |
| SC6 Flood or urban runoff | Sewage, fuel, industrial chemicals, viruses | Chemical mixtures, viruses | X | X | X as a sole barrier | Not sufficient alone |
| SC7 Brackish, sea, industrial or mining water | Salinity and dissolved contaminants | Salt, metals | X | X | X | X |

Every test or field sample records: water-body type, site and season, recent rain, upstream land use, pH, conductivity or TDS, turbidity (NTU), temperature, appearance and odour, E. coli indicator results and suspected chemical hazards, with arsenic, nitrate, lead, cyanotoxin, PFAS, pesticide, salinity or fuel screening as the watershed demands. One sample never stands for a whole class.
