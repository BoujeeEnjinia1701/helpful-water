# Helpful Water C: household gravity filter feasibility assessment

C1 is feasible to build and test as a prototype: every part is bought, sawn or printed, nothing is potted or glued, and the physics checks out on paper (HWC-STM-001, HWC-CAL-001). Feasibility at humanitarian scale rests on two things the paper cannot settle: the bought module's real flux and life, and whether households keep using it.

> **Release limitation.** No prototype is used to treat water for drinking until its pathogen performance, materials safety, integrity, source suitability and claims are validated by qualified laboratories (HWP-CLM-001).

Table 1. Feasibility by aspect.

| Aspect | Assessment | Rating | Risks and notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Head, flow and daily volume met on paper with margin at the clean module and just inside 3 h when fouled | High | Rests on the assumed permeability (HWC-DEC-001 C1); STM S3 |
| Materials | Food-grade HDPE, PP, silicone, EPDM, stainless; untreated timber | High | Certificates for every wetted part (PR-016) |
| Manufacturing and assembly | Saw, drill, spanner, FDM printer; twelve assembly steps | High | Hole positions in tapered bucket walls need care (HWC-BLD-001) |
| Power | None | High | |
| Controls | Manual valve, pinch clamp, tap | High | User discipline on settle and contact times; pictograms |
| Supply | Buckets, timber and fasteners local; module and small fittings imported | Medium | Module resupply about every two years (PR-018); supplier data still needed |
| Pathogen performance | Size exclusion with a large margin; untested | Medium | Accredited testing (HWP-VER-001 TA, TB) decides |
| Adoption | Closed storage and a tap suit households; a 1.27 m fill rim is high | Medium | Persona trials (TH); the Kenya warning (HWP-PRB-001) |
