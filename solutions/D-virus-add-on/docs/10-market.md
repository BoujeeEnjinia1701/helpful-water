# Helpful Water D: chlorine co-treatment add-on market and viability

D does not compete with chlorine tablets; it makes the tablets NGOs already buy easier to use correctly with the A to C filters. Its value is in fewer dose and timing errors, not in cheaper chlorine, so it must stay cheap beside the tablets: an estimated USD 1.40 per cap at scale, against USD 0.0006 per litre of chlorine for a household dosing 20 L buckets. The market context, buyers and benchmarks of the program are in HWP-MKT-001 and are not repeated here. Prices checked 2026-11-11.

## Viability (HWD-CAL-001 section 10)

- Value-engineering target: USD 10 of prototype parts. Estimated cost of the constructable design: USD 9.15 (USD 0.85 under the target), including USD 3.20 of test tablets at retail.
- Cost per litre over a two-year life: about USD 0.0006 for a household of five dosing 20 L buckets (456 tablets a year, USD 5.72 a household a year including the cap at its scale cost); about USD 0.012 for one person dosing 1 L bottles (USD 12.74 a year). At retail tablet prices the personal route costs about USD 0.22 a litre and is not viable as the main route (HWD-DEC-001 D5).
- Consumable: standard household NaDCC tablets; no proprietary refill, so resupply follows the existing humanitarian chain (PR-020). Strip shelf life 5 years.
- Adoption: the program's warning on household filter use (HWP-MKT-001) applies; correct and sustained use is the KPI that matters for D.

## Open route

The cap's drawings are open under CERN-OHL-P-2.0, so regional moulders can make it; the tablets are bought registered products. Helpful Engineering's volunteer network and WASH partners carry the design to pilots after accredited testing of the combined systems (HWP-VER-001 TB, TJ).
