# Helpful Water D: chlorine add-on for viruses STEM check

Verdict: household chlorination with a matched NaDCC tablet (option D1) and with metered 1.25 % hypochlorite (option D2) sit within known science for viruses and bacteria in clear, near-neutral water. At about 2 mg/L in clear water the free chlorine left after 30 minutes is estimated at 1.7 mg/L, giving a CT (residual at the end of the wait x time, the US EPA definition) of about 51 mg min/L, six times the 8 mg min/L that the US EPA table sets for 4-log virus inactivation at 5 C (pH 6 to 9). No virus credit is taken above 5 NTU. Neither option touches *Cryptosporidium* (its CT is about 15,300 mg min/L), so the filter stays essential, as HWP-CLM-001 says. Five details failed as briefed and were fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 (F1 to F6). An independent review on 2026-11-11 refuted five rows and weakened eighteen; six more fixes followed under the same instruction (F7 to F12: a pour-and-stir method for the 20 L bucket, a bottle range on the label, a wider D2 dropper orifice, an O-ring groove with stated tolerances, a 45 minute timer that still reads 30 minutes at 40 C, and no polycarbonate in the well). Two findings go to Amish: wherever A to C put carbon after the wait, the filtered and stored water carries no chlorine residual (S16, D3), and the user has no field check of the residual, so a wrong or weak dose is silent (S43, D7). The erosion feeder (D3) fails on its working principle and is dropped. `stem_check: open` (two fixes awaiting Amish, nineteen Plausible rows that only laboratory tests, supplier data or measured tablets can close). Method: STANDARDS sections 20 and 25.6; numbers from HWD-CAL-001 (`docs/04-calcs/sizing.py`), checks in `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`.

> **Release limitation.** Nothing here is a validated water treatment product. No prototype is used to treat water for drinking until its pathogen performance, materials safety, integrity, source suitability and claims are validated by qualified laboratories (HWP-CLM-001).
