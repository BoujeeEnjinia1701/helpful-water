# Helpful Water D: chlorine add-on for viruses feasibility assessment

D1 is highly feasible on chemistry, materials and supply, because it relies on a long-established household chlorination practice and on tablets already in humanitarian supply; its two engineering unknowns are small (the ball timer's fluid grade and the tablet sizes), and its largest risk sits outside the cap, in the program sequence that removes the residual (HWD-DEC-001 D3). Links: HWD-STM-001, HWD-CAL-001.

Table 1. Feasibility by aspect.

| Aspect | Assessment | Rating | Risks and notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design (chemistry) | 2 mg/L household dose, CT 51 (US EPA definition) against 8 for 4-log virus at 5 C; cold, pH and turbidity rules from WHO-referenced guidance | High | Demand and decay assumed; measured in TJ and TD |
| Design (mechanics) | Cap on a standard PCO 1881 neck, keyed well, screw plug, ball timer in a separate slot | High | Timer fall time depends on fluid grade and wall effect (C2) |
| Materials | PP, silicone, EPDM, PC or PETG, all resistant to dilute chlorine and available in listed grades | High | Printed prototypes are not listed; known potable test water only |
| Manufacturing and assembly | Seven printed parts, one cut tube, one cut sheet; moulding at scale | High | Timer filling with very thick fluid needs care (warm fluid) |
| Power | None | High | None |
| Controls | None; the timer is passive | High | The user must flip it |
| Supply | NaDCC tablets in humanitarian catalogues; silicone fluid and balls from industrial suppliers | High | Tablet sizes and strengths vary by maker and kit (C1) |
| Interfaces | B's neck fits as is; C needs a 28 mm port and a closed outlet during the wait | Medium | C's design in progress (D4) |
| System outcome | No residual after carbon in A to C | Medium | Program decision (D3) |
